# Missing emphasis is the same error as over-reading a quote

## The exchange

Peter wrote:

```
keepers.md is a good example. I should have used _emphasis_ but I meant
it's your job to record whatever you think is relevant to do your job.
```

Claude had read the sentence flat, and had filed `keepers.md` on the wrong side
of an automate/don't-automate split — treating it as a negotiated artefact whose
stability was the point, when it is the gate's own working memory.

## Why

This is the **third** instance in one session of the same underlying error, and
the first two pointed in the opposite direction:

1. Over-read `"considdered_prose"` as character-exact because it arrived in
   quotes.
2. Quoted an aside as though it were a ruling.
3. **Under-read** an unmarked emphasis and missed which word carried the weight.

The symmetry is the lesson. Peter's typography **points** — it marks which words
matter, and sometimes signals that reading between the lines is appropriate.
Errors run both ways: treating a delimiter as an assertion about characters, and
treating its absence as an assertion of flatness. **Absence of a marker is not
evidence of flat emphasis** — the same shape as [[unmarked-is-not-rejected]].

## The rule

Read for **where the weight falls**, not for the markup. When a sentence would
change meaning depending on which word is stressed, and no marker settles it,
that ambiguity is real — resolve it from context, or ask. Do not default to the
flattest reading.
